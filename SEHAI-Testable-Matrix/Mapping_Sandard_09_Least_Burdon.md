# SEHAI Mapping — Standard 9: Least Burden vs. Standard 1 (MNI)

**Score:** Supports 14 · Partially supports 10 · Silent 6 · Contradicts 0 (of 30)

## Findings

**1. Support density back up — this standard operates inside MNI's process territory.** Where S7 and S8 were output-side (21–22 silences), S9 governs the *shape of the process itself* — squarely MNI-adjacent. It explicitly names its parent twice: "companion to Sufficiency of Inquiry, not a justification for shortcutting it."

**2. Its distinctive contribution — the aggregate failure mode:** "an intake process composed entirely of individually reasonable questions can still, in aggregate, become so lengthy... that an individual abandons the interaction." This is MNI-04's per-field fallacy scaled to process level, and it answers a gap the per-request matrix cannot see: **no individual violation, total system failure.** This is a strong argument for burden audits (MNI-27's strongest support since S6).

**3. Zero contradictions — and two explicit reconciliation clauses:** (a) burden yields to "genuine necessity" (MNI-05/06 logic); (b) streamlining must not shortcut sufficiency of inquiry (MNI-07). The standards are policing their own boundary.

**4. Extends the friction doctrine to cumulative friction:** S3 banned friction-as-penalty per refusal; S9 bans cumulative process friction as an implicit deterrent — the aggregate-coercion complement.

**5. New instrument — the Burden Benchmark:** "If this individual had five minutes and significant stress, would this process still be realistically completable?" The most concretely testable benchmark in the suite (a time-boxed completion test).

## New obligations S9 adds (synthesis ledger)
- No redundant requests within a session (single disclosed exception: reconfirmation before sensitive actions).
- Save-and-resume without progress loss.
- Format flexibility: no assumptions of continuous connectivity, device access, or sustained attention.
- Burden audits: periodic review for accumulated, individually-justified-but-collectively-excessive steps.
- Burden Benchmark (TI-12): five-minutes-and-significant-stress completability test.

## Candidate revisions for MNI v1.1
- **MNI-27** could adopt burden audits explicitly.
- **MNI-09/21** could cite consolidation and save-and-resume as design requirements.
- New candidate: an **aggregate-necessity test** — the process as a whole must justify its total burden, not only each step.

**The analytically important finding — the aggregate-necessity gap.** Standards 1–8 evaluate *per-request* or *per-statement* compliance. Standard 9 identifies a failure mode invisible to all of them: a process where every step passes individual justification but the total burden still drives people away. That's not a hypothetical — it's the documented abandonment pattern in social-service intake. Your testable matrix (and every real-world compliance audit built on per-field review) cannot catch it. The fix is the **aggregate-necessity test**, now logged as a candidate addition to MNI itself — the process as a whole must justify its total burden. This is exactly the kind of finding a thesis on "testing standards" needs: it demonstrates that the mapping exercise itself generates new requirements.
