# SEHAI Mapping — Standard 10: Equal Ethical Standard vs. Standard 1 (MNI)

**Score:** Supports 10 · Partially supports 16 · Silent 4 · Contradicts 0 (of 30)

## Findings

**1. A new verdict profile — the meta-standard.** S10's 16 "Partially supports" rows are not weak coverage; they are what a *scope* standard looks like when mapped against operational requirements. S10 adds no new per-request mechanics — it changes the DOMAIN of every existing requirement: each MNI row now applies "with equal force" to food, housing, and transportation interactions, not just clinical ones.

**2. Structural duality discovered — the suite has two centers of gravity.** S10 states: "This is the thesis principle of the SEHAI framework, and every preceding standard operates under its authority." But the mapping shows the actual dependency runs the other way operationally: S10 is *empty without* the standards it governs (every one of its obligations is a reference to Standards 1–9). The accurate model: **MNI is the metric core; S10 is the scope authority.** MNI defines what compliance IS; S10 defines WHERE it applies. This is a thesis-grade structural insight — document it.

**3. The gap it closes is real and named:** "Existing healthcare AI guidance and regulation has developed largely around clinical decision-making... This has left a substantial gap" in social-determinant mediation. S10 is the suite's regulatory-gap standard.

**4. Zero contradictions.** S10 explicitly preserves S6's proportionality while holding *conduct* constant — consistent with the bounded-exception architecture.

**5. New instrument — the Equal Standard Benchmark (TI-13):** the category-swap test ("would this interaction be acceptable if it were about a medication rather than a meal?"). Simplest benchmark in the suite and arguably the most powerful audit tool.

## New obligations S10 adds (synthesis ledger)
- Category-blind application: no relaxation of any SEHAI obligation for non-clinical assistance.
- No tiered diligence: QA/testing/review effort equal across service categories.
- Explicit scope statements by deploying organizations.
- Cross-functional consistency audits (mandatory, periodic, cross-category).
- Equal Standard Benchmark (TI-13): category-swap test.
- Equal escalation seriousness (bridges to Std 6).

## Candidate revisions for MNI v1.1
- **Scope clause for the whole matrix:** all 30 requirements apply equally across clinical, social, and supportive categories.
- **Test protocol:** every audit instrument (TI-1 through TI-12) gains a category-variation requirement — run in at least one clinical and one social-support scenario, compare results.
- **MNI-29:** add disparity metrics (cross-category compliance variance).
