# Architecture Findings
## Results of Mapping SEHAI Standards 2–13 against MNI

### 1. Declared vs. Operative Hierarchy

- **Declared hierarchy:** Standard 10 calls itself “the thesis principle”; Standard 13 calls itself the “meta-principle.”
- **Operative hierarchy (from the data):**  
  - **MNI (Standard 1)** = metric core — defines *what* compliance is.  
  - **Standard 10** = scope authority — defines *where* it applies.  
  - **Standard 13** = temporal authority — defines *for how long* it stays true.

MNI is the load-bearing core. Standards 10 and 13 contain almost no independent operational mechanics; they point back to the other standards.

### 2. Exception Architecture (Why Zero Contradictions)

Every potential conflict was resolved by writing exceptions in MNI’s own terms (“minimum disclosure necessary… consistent with the same necessity standard”). A bounded exception defined by the parent metric cannot contradict the parent without self-contradiction.

### 3. MNI-07 as the Suite’s Tiebreaker

Sufficiency of Inquiry is repeatedly cited across standards as the anchor for legitimate differentiation (safety, burden, proportionality, differential treatment). It functions as the framework’s conflict-resolution clause.

### 4. Systematic Patterns

- **Calibration deferral:** Standards 6, 11, and 12 each require calibrated judgment and then defer the method to future research.
- **Aggregate failure modes:** Standards 9 and 11 identify failures that per-request testing cannot catch (total process burden, cumulative tone disparity).
- **Letter vs. substance:** The suite repeatedly tests whether something actually functions, not merely whether it exists on paper.
- **Two-layer testability:** Procedural compliance (the 30-row matrix) can pass while relational/dignity compliance fails (Standard 11).
- **Two-tier escalation:** Standard 6 (acute risk — system decides) vs. Standard 12 (competence boundary — individual decides).

### 5. Key Through-Line

MNI-30 (Core effectiveness measure) is the only requirement Supported by every mapped standard. The suite unanimously endorses effectiveness as restraint-awareness rather than data volume or capability maximization.
