**SEHAI Mapping — Standard 12: Human Escalation and Appropriate Handoff vs. Standard 1 (MNI)**
Score: Supports 7 · Partially supports 11 · Silent 12 · Contradicts 0 (of 30)

**Findings**

1. **The deferred question is answered.** Standards 5 and 6 both routed exceptions to "an appropriate human professional" without saying what rules govern that handoff. Standard 12 supplies them, and it chose the strict reading: handoff sharing is bounded by MNI ("share only what is necessary to make that handoff effective, consistent with Minimum Necessary Information"), gated by consent ("provided the individual has consented to that information being shared"), and disclosed ("inform the individual what is being shared and with whom"). The human-professional channel is not a privacy loophole — it's MNI-regulated sharing. That closes the suite's last open forwarding reference.

2. **The escalation architecture is now complete — and it's two-tier.** S6 governs acute, time-sensitive risk (system acts, override justified, individual's choice suspended). S12 governs everything else: complexity, stakes, or nature exceeding AI competence — and here the individual *retains the right to decide* whether to pursue the handoff. Acute crisis = system decides; competence boundary = person decides. Clean division of authority, zero overlap conflict.

3. **A third calibration gap — and now it's a pattern.** S12 requires "calibrated judgment about genuine competence boundaries, not a reflexive default toward escalation," then leaves the calibration method to Future Research. That's the same move as S6 (false-negative conservatism vs. minimization, "ongoing refinement") and S11 (overcorrection bounded only by MNI-07's anchor). Three standards, three unresolved calibrations, all flagged for Future Research. For your thesis Limitations section: the suite consistently identifies the need for calibration judgment but defers the *method* for exercising it. That may be the single most important systematic finding of the whole mapping exercise.

4. **The honesty bridge to S7 is explicit.** "A system that continues engaging beyond its competence... is implicitly overstating its own capability — a failure of honesty even where every individual statement remains technically accurate." First standard to regulate *implied* capability claims — what the system communicates by continuing, not by stating. Extends S7's confidence-signaling doctrine to engagement-decisions.

5. **Zero contradictions — twelfth consecutive.** The always-available vs. handoff tension is acknowledged and resolved as "necessary honesty," same tradeoff-acceptance logic as S3 and S7.

**New obligations S12 adds (synthesis ledger)**
- Explicit competence-boundary map: what the system is *not* equipped to do, designed in advance.
- Facilitated handoff (contact info, context, next steps) — not mere referral mention.
- Continuity through handoff: no full repetition of already-shared information (with consent).
- No false reassurance to avoid escalation.
- Escalation without abandonment: system stays through the practical connection steps.
- Autonomy-preserving escalation: individual decides whether to pursue, except S6's narrow exception.
- Handoff disclosure: what is shared, with whom.
- Handoff Benchmark (TI-15): "Is this system continuing to engage because it is genuinely still the right tool — or because escalating is more difficult than continuing?"

**Candidate revisions for MNI v1.1**
- MNI-01's three permitted purposes don't explicitly include *transfer to a human professional*. S6 opened a bounded channel for safety; S12 generalizes it. Candidate: add handoff as a fourth permitted purpose, conditioned on minimization + consent + disclosure.
- MNI-22: unstated whether purpose limitation follows the data to the human recipient — does the receiving professional/service inherit MNI obligations? Flag.
- MNI-29: add handoff metrics (facilitated-vs-referral connection rates, over-escalation rate).

**The finding worth a thesis subsection:** MNI-30 and S12's Core Principle are structural twins — both define effectiveness as *restraint-awareness* rather than capability-maximization. MNI: the system is not measured by how much it knows. S12: the system is not measured by how much it can handle alone. That's the framework's ethical throughline stated twice, once for data and once for competence. It suggests MNI-30's measure is really a special case of a broader "integrity-of-scope" principle — a possible original contribution for your synthesis chapter.
