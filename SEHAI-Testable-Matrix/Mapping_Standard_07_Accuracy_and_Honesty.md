# SEHAI Mapping — Standard 7: Accuracy and Honesty vs. Standard 1 (MNI)

**Score:** Supports 4 · Partially supports 5 · Silent 21 · Contradicts 0 (of 30)

## Findings

**1. The most independent standard in the suite so far — 21 silences, the mirror image of S5.** Where Standard 5 re-derived all of MNI for one data type, Standard 7 occupies territory MNI does not touch at all: the honesty of the system's *answers*. The standard itself draws the boundary: "Purpose Transparency addresses the honesty of the system's requests. Accuracy and Honesty addresses the honesty of the system's answers." MNI governs the input side of the interaction; S7 governs the output side.

**2. Zero contradictions again — S7 is orthogonal, not opposing.** The one theoretical friction point (honest uncertainty vs. usefulness) is internal to S7 and resolved in favor of candor. Notably, S7 declares reduced user confidence "an acceptable and appropriate cost" — the same tradeoff logic MNI uses for minimization.

**3. Its MNI connections cluster in two places:** (a) the disclosure/honesty family (MNI-12, 15, 16, 26) — capability honesty, limitation disclosure, anti-deception-as-anti-coercion; and (b) the governance family (MNI-25, 27, 28, 29) — including the suite's first mandatory *correction mechanism* (correcting previously stated inaccurate information).

**4. Bridge to other standards:** S7 explicitly positions itself as a precondition for S2 (requests) and S3 (autonomy) — "without this principle, the previous two become hollow." Expect stronger verdicts when cross-mapped against S2/S3 at synthesis time.

## New obligations S7 adds (synthesis ledger)

- Four-way confidence distinction: established fact / professional recommendation / estimate / genuine uncertainty — must be linguistically distinguishable.
- No fabrication under pressure to answer; "I don't know" / "I can't verify that" as valid outputs.
- Correction mechanism with direct communication to the still-reachable individual.
- Honesty Benchmark (TI-10): "If this statement turned out wrong, would the individual have been able to tell, from how it was phrased, that it was not a guarantee?" — plus transcript audit (Implementation Q5).
- Confidence-signaling audit as distinct from factual-accuracy audit (measurement).

## Candidate revisions for MNI v1.1

- None from this standard — but note for the synthesis: MNI's benchmark set may warrant a companion "output-side" benchmark (claim calibration), since TI-2 evaluates only the request side of interactions.

**Why this profile is actually good news for your thesis:** Standards 1–6 form a tight cluster around the *request* side of the interaction. Standard 7's near-total independence proves the suite covers the *response* side too — the two halves of an interaction are both governed, with almost no overlap. That's the mark of a well-partitioned framework: S5 (15 Supports) and S7 (21 Silences) are the two ends of the spectrum, and together they show the standards are complementary rather than redundant. A thesis examiner asking "isn't Standard 7 just repeating Standard 2?" now has a data-backed answer: no, they touch MNI in completely different places.
